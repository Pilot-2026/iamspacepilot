Privacy and terms of use

iamSpacePilot is a free game you play in the browser. This page explains which data the game uses, what happens when you play online and talk on the voice radio, and the rules and limits of liability that apply when you use it.

In short

You can fly solo without giving any personal data. No cookies for tracking, no advertising. We count visits for statistics, with shortened IP addresses only (never the full address).

If you post a score, your callsign and score are public on the leaderboard.

If you play online, other pilots see your callsign. If you talk on the radio, everyone in that game hears you live, including strangers. We do not record the radio.

Be respectful. You use the online features and the radio at your own risk. We are not responsible for what other pilots say or do.

1. Who is responsible

iamSpacePilot is a non-commercial game by Andreas Schmölzer (pilotFilm). Name, address and contact details are in the Impressum (legal notice). Please use those contact details for any question about this page or about your data.

2. Data the game uses

2.1 Visiting the website (hosting)

The game is hosted by Netlify, Inc. (USA). When you open the page, the hosting provider processes technical data that every browser sends, such as your IP address, the time, the pages and files requested, and your browser and operating system. This is needed to deliver the game and keep it secure. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in running a secure website (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

2.2 Data stored only on your device

The game keeps your callsign, your settings, your local highscores and a random pilot key in your browser's local storage. This data stays on your device unless you post a score or play online (see below). You can delete it at any time by clearing your browser's site data. The game does not use cookies for tracking.

2.3 Online leaderboard

When you finish a solo flight with a callsign, the game sends your callsign, a random pilot key (it does not contain your name), the level, your score, your flight time and a recording of your control inputs to our server. The server replays the flight to check the score and stores it in Google Firebase (Cloud Firestore, provided by Google). Your callsign and score are shown publicly on the leaderboard. Legal basis: providing the leaderboard you choose to take part in (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR) and our legitimate interest in fair, cheat-free scores (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

2.4 Online games and the voice radio

Online games run through LiveKit Cloud (LiveKit, Inc., USA). When you host or join a game, your callsign, your ship and the flight data of your glider (position, walls, shots) are sent to the other pilots in that game. Public games appear in the list of games with the host's callsign, the level and the number of pilots.

Voice radio: if you allow the microphone and talk (push to talk, or open mic if you switch it on), your voice is sent live to every pilot in the game. That may include people you do not know. We do not record, store or listen to the radio. LiveKit processes your IP address and connection data to transmit audio and game data. Other pilots could record what you say with their own devices; this is not allowed (see section 5), but we cannot technically prevent it.

Legal basis: providing the online game you choose to join (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR) and your consent to using the microphone (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR). You can withdraw your consent at any time by not using the radio, switching the microphone off, leaving the game or blocking microphone access in your browser.

2.5 Service providers and transfers outside the EU

We use these processors: Netlify, Inc. (hosting, USA), Google (Firebase / Cloud Firestore, leaderboard data) and LiveKit, Inc. (online games and voice, USA). Data may be processed in the USA. Such transfers are based on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework where the provider is certified, or on the EU Standard Contractual Clauses. Their own privacy policies apply: Netlify, Google Firebase, LiveKit.

2.6 How long data is kept

Leaderboard entries are kept as long as the game and its leaderboard exist, unless you ask us to delete them. Game sessions and radio audio are not stored after a game ends. Technical logs are kept by the hosting and service providers for a short time according to their own rules. Data on your device stays until you delete it.

2.7 Visit statistics

To know how often and from where the game is played, every page load and every flight adds to counters: the total, the day, the country and city, the type of device (phone, tablet or computer), the website you came from (only its name, e.g. google.com) and the level flown. Country and city are worked out by our hosting provider from the connection at that moment.

To count how many different people visit and how many come back, each visit also updates one record per visitor. It is identified by a pseudonymous key, a hash of your IP address and your browser with a secret value, which cannot be turned back into the address. The record keeps your IP address shortened (the last part removed, e.g. 84.115.12.x), country, city, device, the day of your first and last visit and how many visits. Your full IP address is never stored. Nothing is stored on your device for this, and the records are not combined with your callsign or anything else. Only the operator can see the list of visitors.

Legal basis: our legitimate interest in knowing how and where the game is used and in recognising misuse (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). The statistics are kept permanently, as long as the game exists. You can object at any time and ask us to delete your record (contact via the Impressum; as we cannot identify you ourselves, tell us your network and browser or visit from the same device so we can find the key). The data is stored in Google Firebase (see 2.5).

3. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access your data, to have it corrected or deleted, to restrict its processing, to data portability, to object to processing based on legitimate interest, and to withdraw a consent at any time with effect for the future. To use these rights, for example to have a callsign and its scores deleted from the leaderboard, contact us using the details in the Impressum. Because we do not know who is behind a callsign, we may ask you to prove that it is yours (for example by using the browser in which you created it).

You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection authority. In Austria this is the Österreichische Datenschutzbehörde, Barichgasse 40–42, 1030 Wien, www.dsb.gv.at.

4. Children and young people

The online games and the voice radio connect you with people you do not know. The voice radio is meant for pilots aged 16 and over. If you are younger, you may only use the online features and the radio with the permission of a parent or guardian. We recommend that younger players fly solo, or play online only with friends and with a grown-up nearby. Parents: the radio can be avoided entirely by not allowing the microphone, and any pilot can be muted in the game.

5. Rules for online games and the voice radio

By playing online you agree to these rules:

  • Be respectful. No insults, harassment, threats, bullying, discrimination, hate speech, sexual content or any other unlawful content, neither on the radio nor in your callsign.
  • Do not share personal data, neither your own (address, phone number, school, passwords …) nor anyone else's.
  • Do not record, stream or publish what other pilots say without their consent.
  • Do not pretend to be someone else, and do not use a real person's name as your callsign unless it is your own.
  • No cheating, hacking, spamming, or attacks on the game, its servers or other players.
  • No advertising and no commercial use of the radio.

If someone breaks the rules: mute them, leave the game, and tell us via the contact details in the Impressum. In case of crimes, contact the police. We may remove callsigns and scores and block access to the online features for anyone who breaks these rules. The live radio is not moderated in real time.

7. Health

The game contains flashing lights, fast motion and changing patterns. A very small number of people may have seizures (photosensitive epilepsy) when exposed to such images. If you or anyone in your family has had epilepsy or seizures, ask a doctor before playing. Stop playing at once if you feel dizzy, have blurred vision, twitching, or any other discomfort. Take regular breaks, and play in a well-lit room.
